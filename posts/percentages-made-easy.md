Percentages made easy start with one idea: "percent" literally means "per hundred", so 25% means 25 out of every 100. Once that idea clicks, every percentage problem becomes a fraction problem in disguise — and fraction problems are far easier than they look.

## Percentages Made Easy: What "Percent" Really Means

The symbol % means "out of 100". So 50% means 50 out of 100, 25% means 25 out of 100, and 100% means all of it. Picture a grid of 100 small squares. Shading 25 of them shows 25%. Shading all 100 shows 100%. Shading 150 squares would need a second grid, which is why percentages above 100% mean "more than the whole" — possible for growth, but not for a share of one thing.

Because percent means "per hundred", every percentage is a fraction with 100 on the bottom. The percentage 60% is the fraction 60/100, which simplifies to 3/5. The percentage 5% is 5/100, which simplifies to 1/20. Converting between percentages, fractions, and decimals is mostly a matter of moving between these three forms of the same number.

A handy anchor set is worth memorising: 50% = 1/2 = 0.5, 25% = 1/4 = 0.25, 75% = 3/4 = 0.75, 10% = 1/10 = 0.1, and 20% = 1/5 = 0.2. These five anchors solve a surprising number of everyday problems without any calculator.

## Converting Between Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages

To turn a fraction into a percentage, divide the numerator by the denominator and multiply by 100. For 3/5: 3 divided by 5 is 0.6, and 0.6 times 100 is 60, so 3/5 = 60%. To turn a decimal into a percentage, multiply by 100: 0.35 becomes 35%. To turn a percentage into a decimal, divide by 100: 45% becomes 0.45.

Going the other way is just as mechanical. To turn a percentage into a fraction, write it over 100 and simplify: 40% = 40/100 = 2/5. To turn a percentage into a decimal, drop the % sign and move the decimal point two places left: 72% = 0.72.

Try a less friendly example: what is 7/8 as a percentage? Divide 7 by 8 to get 0.875, then multiply by 100 to get 87.5%. The decimal point trick works in both directions and is the fastest route once you trust it: multiply by 100 to go toward percent, divide by 100 to come back.

## Worked Example: Finding a Percentage of a Number

To find a percentage of a number, convert the percentage to a fraction or decimal, then multiply. Find 25% of 80. Since 25% = 1/4, divide 80 by 4 to get 20. So 25% of 80 is 20. Alternatively, 25% = 0.25, and 0.25 times 80 is also 20.

For a trickier percentage, the decimal route is usually easiest. Find 15% of 200. Convert 15% to 0.15, then multiply: 0.15 times 200 = 30. Or use the 10% trick: 10% of 200 is 20, and 5% is half of that, 10, so 15% is 20 + 10 = 30. Building a percentage from 10% and 5% chunks is a powerful mental-math strategy.

Check that answers make sense. Since 15% is less than 50%, the answer must be less than half of 200 (100). Since 15% is more than 10%, the answer must be more than 20. The answer 30 fits both checks.

## Worked Example: Discounts and Sale Prices

Discount problems ask for two things: how much you save, and what you actually pay. A jacket costs 60 and is reduced by 40%. First find the saving: 40% of 60. Convert 40% to 0.4 and multiply: 0.4 times 60 = 24. So you save 24. The sale price is 60 minus 24, which is 36.

There is a faster route: if 40% is taken off, you pay the remaining 60%. So compute 60% of 60 directly: 0.6 times 60 = 36. Both routes agree, which is reassuring. Use whichever feels more natural, and use the other as a check.

Another example: a book costs 45 with a 20% discount. The discount is 0.2 times 45 = 9, so the sale price is 45 minus 9 = 36. Or pay 80%: 0.8 times 45 = 36. Notice how the "pay the rest" method needs only one multiplication — a handy shortcut in a shop.

## Worked Example: Percentage Increase and Decrease

Percentage change compares a new value with an old one. A plant grows from 50 cm to 65 cm. The increase is 65 minus 50 = 15 cm. To express this as a percentage of the starting height, divide the increase by the original value and multiply by 100: 15 divided by 50 = 0.3, and 0.3 times 100 = 30%. The plant grew by 30%.

The rule for any percentage change is: (change divided by original) times 100. The "original" is always the starting value, never the new one. If a price falls from 80 to 60, the decrease is 20, and 20 divided by 80 is 0.25, so the price fell by 25%. If a score rises from 40 to 50, the increase is 10, and 10 divided by 40 is 0.25, so the score rose by 25%.

A common trap: an increase of 50% followed by a decrease of 50% does not return you to the start. Start with 100. A 50% increase gives 150. A 50% decrease of 150 takes away 75, leaving 75. The second percentage acts on a different, larger base.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid with Percentages

The biggest mistake is confusing "percentage points" with "percent". If a pass rate rises from 40% to 50%, it has risen by 10 percentage points, but the relative increase is 10 divided by 40 = 25%. Both statements are correct; they measure different things. In everyday talk, people usually mean percentage points, but in maths problems, read the wording carefully.

Another mistake is using the wrong base for a percentage change. Always divide by the original value. A rise from 40 to 60 is a 50% increase (20 divided by 40), not a 33% increase (20 divided by 60). Ask yourself: "Percentage of what?" The answer is almost always "of the starting value".

A third mistake is treating a percentage as a fixed amount. Ten percent of 200 is 20, but 10% of 50 is only 5. The percentage tells you the share; the base tells you the size. Always identify the base before calculating.

## FAQ

### What does percent actually mean?

Percent means "per hundred". So 25% means 25 out of every 100, and 100% means all of it. The symbol % is shorthand for "divided by 100", which is why every percentage can be written as a fraction with 100 on the bottom.

### How do you convert a fraction to a percentage?

Divide the numerator by the denominator, then multiply by 100. For 3/5, 3 divided by 5 is 0.6, and 0.6 times 100 is 60, so 3/5 = 60%. For a decimal, just multiply by 100: 0.35 becomes 35%.

### How do you find a percentage of a number?

Convert the percentage to a decimal or fraction, then multiply. For 15% of 200, use 0.15 times 200 = 30. A useful shortcut: 10% is the number divided by 10, and 5% is half of that, so 15% is 10% plus 5%.

### What is the difference between a percentage and a percentage point?

A percentage point is the simple difference between two percentages. If a rate rises from 40% to 50%, that is a 10-percentage-point rise, but a 25% relative increase (10 divided by 40). The two phrases measure different things, so check which one a question asks for.

### Can a percentage be more than 100%?

Yes. Percentages above 100% mean "more than the whole", which makes sense for growth: a value that doubles has grown by 100%, and one that triples has grown by 200%. For a share of a single thing, though, the maximum is 100%.
