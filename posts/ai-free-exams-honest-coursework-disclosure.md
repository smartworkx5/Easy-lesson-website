Exam halls stay AI-free while coursework asks for honest disclosure. That is the picture emerging from reports in October 2026, and it captures a simple principle: ai free exams coursework disclosure means proving what you can do on your own in exams, and being transparent about any help you used in coursework. Exams are designed to measure your independent thinking, while coursework tasks are set so you still show your own judgement even when AI is permitted. This guide explains the rule, why it exists, and how to follow it without stress.

## AI Free Exams Coursework Disclosure Explained

The basic rule has two halves. In a proctored exam, no AI is allowed at all. You sit down with the paper, your own knowledge, and nothing else. In coursework, AI may be permitted, but you must state how you used it. According to a report published in October 2026, no separate test of AI-free ability is planned beyond this arrangement. The exam itself is the check that you can work without assistance.

Disclosure is not a confession. It is a short, factual note that says what the tool did and what you did. For example: "I used an AI tool to brainstorm three possible essay structures, then chose one and wrote every paragraph myself." That kind of honesty protects you. A teacher who knows exactly how AI was used can judge your thinking fairly, and you never have to worry that a good grade rests on hidden help.

## Why Exam Halls Stay AI-Free

Exams are designed to measure what a student can do independently. That is the reported rationale behind keeping them AI-free. A qualification only means something if it reflects your own knowledge, writing, reasoning, and problem solving. If an AI tool supplied the answers, the grade would describe the tool, not the student.

There is a practical reason too. Life after school keeps asking you to think without a chatbot beside you: job interviews, practical tasks, conversations, and decisions under pressure. Exams train that independence. They also keep things fair. Every student faces the same conditions, the same time limit, and the same paper. Nobody gains an advantage from a better tool or a faster connection.

Some students worry that AI-free exams are old-fashioned. A better way to see them is as a skills check. The coursework shows what you can produce with help and good judgement; the exam shows what you truly carry in your head. Both matter, and both are part of how your learning is judged.

## How to Declare AI Use Honestly in Coursework

Honest disclosure is a habit, and it gets easier with practice. Start by reading the task instructions carefully. They should say whether AI is allowed, and for what. If the instructions are silent, ask your teacher before you use a tool. Never assume permission.

When you do use AI, keep a short log as you go. Note the date, the tool's purpose, and what it produced. Did it suggest an outline? Did it check your grammar? Did it explain a concept you then applied yourself? Write it down in plain language. At the end of the assignment, add a disclosure note of two or three sentences covering those points.

Be specific rather than vague. "I used AI" tells the reader almost nothing. "I used an AI tool to rephrase two sentences in my introduction and to check my spelling" tells the reader everything. Specificity is what makes disclosure trustworthy. It also helps you reflect: when you list what the tool did, you can see clearly what remains your own work.

## Skills to Practise Without AI

Because exams stay AI-free, you need regular practice working without assistance. Set aside at least one study session each week where no AI tool is open. Write an essay paragraph from memory. Solve problems with only your textbook and notes. Explain a concept aloud to a friend or family member without looking anything up.

Focus on the skills exams actually test: recall, reasoning, writing under time pressure, and problem solving. Flashcards and self-quizzing build recall. Timed practice papers build speed and calm. Writing short summaries from memory builds the ability to organise ideas quickly. These are the skills that carry you through an exam hall, and no tool can practise them for you.

Also practise reading questions carefully. Many exam marks are lost not to ignorance but to misreading. Underline the command words in a question, such as explain, compare, or evaluate, and check that your answer does what the question asks. That habit is worth more than any shortcut.

## A Simple Disclosure Note You Can Adapt

A good disclosure note has three parts: what you used, what it did, and what you did yourself. Here is a pattern you can adapt: "I used an AI tool to [purpose, for example brainstorm ideas]. It produced [what it gave you]. I then [what you did yourself, for example selected, rewrote, checked, and expanded the ideas in my own words]." Keep it short and factual.

Never claim AI-generated text as your own writing. If a tool drafted a paragraph, either rewrite it fully in your own words and say so, or leave it out. And always verify anything a tool tells you against your textbook. AI can make mistakes, invent facts, and sound confident while being wrong. Your textbook is the authority for your course, so check solutions and claims against it before you submit.

If you are ever unsure whether a particular use of AI is allowed, the safest move is to ask your teacher and follow the answer. Teachers would rather answer a question beforehand than investigate a problem afterwards. Honesty is always the lower-stress option.

## FAQ

### Can I use AI during a proctored exam?

No. Proctored exams do not allow AI. You should practise working without AI so that exam conditions feel familiar rather than frightening.

### How do I disclose AI use in coursework?

Write two or three factual sentences saying what the tool did and what you did yourself. Be specific, and attach the note to your assignment as your teacher directs.

### What if my teacher says no AI at all for a task?

Then do the task entirely yourself. Follow the instructions for that task, even if other tasks allow AI. When in doubt, ask before you start.

### Does disclosing AI use lower my grade?

Honest disclosure itself is not a penalty. Teachers judge the thinking, judgement, and understanding you show in the work. Hiding AI use, by contrast, can create serious problems.

## Conclusion

AI-free exams and honest coursework disclosure work together. Exams prove what you can do alone, and disclosure keeps coursework honest when help is allowed. Practise unaided skills every week, keep a short log of any AI help, and write a clear disclosure note with each assignment. That routine protects your grades, your learning, and your confidence.
