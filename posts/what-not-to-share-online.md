Knowing what not to share online is one of the most important lessons for any student, because once something is posted, you lose control of it. A photo, a location, or a small personal detail can be copied, reshared, and saved by people you never intended to reach, and deleting the original rarely deletes every copy. This does not mean you should avoid the internet. It means you should share with intention: choose what goes public, keep private details private, and think about who might see a post years from now. The rules below are simple, and following them protects your safety, your privacy, and your future self.

## The never list: what not to share online

Some details should never be shared publicly, no matter how harmless they seem. Your full name combined with your school, your home address, your phone number, and your live location all belong on the never list, because together they tell a stranger exactly where to find you. Passwords and login codes are never for sharing, even with friends, and private photos, especially photos of yourself or others in private moments, should never be posted or sent to someone you do not fully trust. Seemingly small clues add up too: a school uniform in a photo, a timetable on a desk, or a geotag on a picture can reveal your school and your routine. Before posting, look at the whole image, not just the subject, and check the background, the caption, and the location tag for details you did not mean to share.

## Why Location and Routine Details Are Risky

Location details feel harmless because you share them with friends, but public posts reach far beyond your friend list. A live location, a check-in at a regular place, or a photo posted from somewhere in real time tells anyone watching exactly where you are right now. Repeated posts build a pattern: the same place at the same time each week reveals your routine, and routines are exactly what someone with bad intentions looks for. A safer habit is to post about a place after you have left it, without tagging its exact address, and to turn off location tagging on your photos and posts. Keep your daily schedule, such as when you leave home and when you return, entirely off public posts. Sharing memories is wonderful, but sharing them a little later, without a live location, keeps the memory and removes the risk.

## Thinking Ahead: Your Future Self Is Watching

One of the hardest parts of sharing wisely is remembering that the internet keeps things for a very long time. A joke, a rant, or an embarrassing photo that seems funny at thirteen can resurface years later when you apply for a job, a course, or a leadership role. Future readers will not know the context or the joke behind it, and they may judge you on that one post alone. Before you post something emotional, ask whether you would be comfortable with a teacher, a future employer, or your own family reading it in five years. If the answer is no, save it privately instead, or do not post it at all. This is not about being boring. It is about making sure the story the internet tells about you is one you chose, not one you posted in a moment and later regretted.

## FAQ

### Is it safe to share my first name online?

A first name on its own is usually low risk, but combined with your school, a photo, or a location it becomes identifying.

### What should I do if I already shared something private?

Delete it as soon as you can, then tell a trusted adult, especially if it includes your location, a password, or a private photo. Deleting does not guarantee every copy is gone, so an adult can help you report the post or ask a platform to remove reshares.

### Can I share photos if my account is private?

A private account reduces the audience, but it does not make sharing safe. Followers can still screenshot and reshare, and private accounts are not immune to leaks.

### Why should I ask before posting photos of friends?

Because the photo is their information too, and they may have their own reasons for not wanting it public. Asking shows respect, protects their privacy, and avoids arguments later.

### Are location tags on old posts still a problem?

They can be, because old posts still reveal patterns and places connected to you.

## Conclusion

Sharing online is part of modern life, and it should stay enjoyable. The rule is simple: share the fun, keep the private details private, ask before sharing others, and think about your future self before you post.
